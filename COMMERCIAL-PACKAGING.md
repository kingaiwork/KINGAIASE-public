# KING AI — Commercial Packaging Framework

**Business contact:** vip@kingai.work

KING AI uses value-based packaging rather than forcing every product into one identical subscription model. This document explains how products can be commercialized without inventing prices, customer counts or capabilities that have not been released.

## Packaging principles

1. **Useful before upsell** — a product should solve a real problem before asking the user to understand the wider ecosystem.
2. **Charge for depth, not the AI label** — paid value should come from workflow depth, continuity, governance, support, collaboration, data delivery, premium content or managed service.
3. **Different products may need different models** — productivity, security, infrastructure, intelligence and entertainment have different economic logic.
4. **Enterprise value requires governance** — administration, auditability, deployment support, access control and contractual responsibility are real enterprise features.
5. **Roadmap is not packaging** — a future feature is not part of a sellable plan until availability is verified.

## Public packaging ladder

### Free / Discovery
Designed to demonstrate immediate value and reduce adoption friction.

Typical value:
- browser utilities;
- selected public product experiences;
- public documentation and demos;
- public Global Intelligence views and methodology;
- limited local-first or guest experiences where supported.

Primary business purpose: acquisition, trust and learning.

### Individual Premium
For people who receive recurring personal value.

Potential value:
- higher limits or deeper workflows;
- premium productivity;
- stronger continuity and personalization;
- premium security features;
- premium digital-life content;
- expanded convenience and support.

Availability depends on each product.

### Professional
For creators, analysts, operators and advanced individual users.

Potential value:
- deeper productivity workflows;
- professional monitoring;
- advanced exports;
- recurring briefs;
- operational tooling;
- priority support;
- workflow automation under explicit authority.

### Team / Business
For organizations that need shared workflows.

Potential value:
- team workspaces;
- shared knowledge;
- customer-support and lead workflows;
- role-based access;
- shared operations;
- business administration;
- business support and onboarding.

### Enterprise
For customers that require stronger governance, deployment support and accountability.

Potential value:
- centralized administration;
- enterprise identity and entitlement integration;
- auditability;
- policy and approval controls;
- deployment assistance;
- private/hybrid options where formally supported;
- service-level and support arrangements;
- data, integration and governance work.

### Managed Services
For customers that want outcomes and operational support rather than software alone.

Potential categories:
- managed infrastructure operations;
- security operations support;
- customer-workflow implementation;
- ongoing automation maintenance;
- custom intelligence monitoring;
- implementation and integration services.

### Strategic / OEM / Distribution
For platform, device, infrastructure, channel and ecosystem partners.

Potential structures:
- distribution agreements;
- OEM/device relationships;
- infrastructure partnerships;
- co-selling;
- embedded product integrations;
- data or content licensing where appropriate;
- strategic co-development.

## What should justify a price increase

A higher-priced tier should correspond to a higher-value outcome, such as:
- more recurring time saved;
- more users or managed environments;
- stronger governance;
- better support;
- deeper integrations;
- greater data/history depth;
- premium content;
- managed responsibility;
- reduced operational burden.

It should not be justified only by token volume or by moving basic usability behind artificial restrictions.

## Pilot-to-contract path for business

A healthy commercial motion is:

**Problem definition → bounded pilot → baseline measurement → governed production scope → support/integration agreement → expansion only after value is proven.**

Good pilot metrics can include handling time, completion rate, response quality, lead capture, operator hours saved, incident resolution time, workflow reliability or research time saved, depending on product.

## Pricing integrity

This public document deliberately does not publish fictional list prices, discounts, ARR assumptions or unit economics. Exact pricing should be introduced only when a product has a defined package, support scope, cost model and verified availability.
