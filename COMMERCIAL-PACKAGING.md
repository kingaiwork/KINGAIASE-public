# KING AI — Commercial Packaging Framework

**Business contact:** vip@kingai.work

KING AI packages value around work and responsibility, not around the AI label itself. The customer path should make sense whether the customer is one person delegating one recurring task or an organization building a governed AI workforce.

This framework does not invent list prices, customer counts, discounts or unreleased capability.

## Commercial idea in one sentence

**Start with one useful outcome. Add AI workers, shared workflows, governance and deployment depth only when each layer earns its place.**

## Packaging principles

1. **Lead with the job to be done** — the customer should understand what work disappears or improves before reading about models, memory or architecture.
2. **Useful before upsell** — a public or entry product should solve a real problem on its own.
3. **Charge for recurring value** — workflow depth, continuity, saved time, collaboration, governance, integration, support, data delivery and managed responsibility justify paid tiers better than the word “AI.”
4. **Package AI workers as roles** — business value becomes clearer when an AI worker has a job, approved knowledge, tools, permissions, approval rules and measurable outcomes.
5. **Enterprise is about responsibility** — administration, auditability, identity, policy, deployment, integration, support and contractual scope are real enterprise features.
6. **Different products keep different economics** — security, productivity, infrastructure, intelligence and entertainment should not be forced into one identical usage meter.
7. **Roadmap is not a sellable entitlement** — future capability is not included in a commercial package until availability is verified.

## The customer growth path

### 1. Free / Discovery — solve something now

Designed to create immediate value with minimal friction.

Typical value:
- 138 browser utilities;
- selected public product experiences;
- public documentation, demos and status;
- public Global Intelligence views and methodology;
- supported guest or local-first experiences.

Commercial purpose:

**Acquire trust by solving a real task before asking the customer to buy a platform.**

### 2. Pro / Individual — one persistent AI work partner

For people who receive recurring value from continuity and richer workflows.

Potential paid value where formally available:
- deeper personal workflows;
- higher limits;
- API access;
- richer productivity;
- stronger continuity and personalization;
- premium security or digital-life capability;
- convenience and support.

The customer story is simple:

**“I no longer start the same work from zero every day.”**

### 3. Business — build an AI team

For companies that want to delegate repeatable work while keeping authority visible.

Potential value:
- organizations and shared workspaces;
- multiple AI roles;
- shared approved knowledge;
- role-based access;
- approval paths;
- budgets and limits;
- customer-support, lead and CRM workflows;
- operations workflows;
- shared reporting and audit;
- onboarding and implementation support.

A business AI worker should be explainable through:

**Role · Goal · Knowledge · Tools · Memory · Permission · Budget · Approval · KPI · Audit**

The customer story is:

**“We added AI capacity without giving a black box unlimited authority.”**

### 4. Enterprise — govern an AI company

For organizations that require stronger control, integration, private deployment patterns and accountability.

Potential value:
- centralized administration;
- enterprise identity and entitlement integration;
- organization-wide policy;
- advanced auditability;
- approval and segregation-of-duty controls;
- private/hybrid deployment options where formally supported;
- integration with existing data and systems;
- agreed support and service scope;
- deployment assistance;
- governance and operating-model design.

The customer story is:

**“AI becomes part of the operating model, not an unmanaged collection of tools.”**

### 5. Managed Services — buy the outcome, not the software project

For customers that want KING AI / approved implementation partners to take defined operational responsibility.

Potential categories:
- managed infrastructure operations;
- security operations support;
- customer-workflow implementation;
- automation maintenance;
- custom intelligence monitoring;
- integration and migration work;
- AI-workforce setup and governance design.

### 6. Strategic / OEM / Distribution

For platform, device, infrastructure, channel and ecosystem partners.

Potential structures:
- distribution agreements;
- OEM/device relationships;
- infrastructure partnerships;
- co-selling;
- embedded product integrations;
- data/content licensing where appropriate;
- strategic co-development.

## Cross-product commercial journey

The strongest ecosystem motion is not “sell all products.” It is:

**Discover → solve → return → trust → delegate repeat work → build history → adopt the next relevant product → expand only after value is proven.**

Examples:

- A Tools user solves a one-off task; repeated work points toward Office or an Agent workflow.
- A Customer OS user starts with support; recurring lead and follow-up work can justify deeper business automation.
- An OPS customer starts with visibility; governed automation and managed operations deepen the relationship when operational value is proven.
- A Personal user starts with one persistent workflow; stronger continuity can make additional products useful later.

## What should justify a higher price

A higher-priced tier should correspond to a higher-value outcome such as:
- more recurring time saved;
- more AI roles or users;
- more managed environments;
- stronger governance;
- better support;
- deeper integrations;
- greater history/data depth;
- premium content;
- managed responsibility;
- reduced operational burden;
- measurable improvement in a business process.

It should not be justified only by token volume or artificial restrictions on basic usability.

## Business pilot-to-contract motion

A healthy motion is:

**Problem definition → bounded pilot → baseline measurement → governed production scope → support/integration agreement → expansion after measured value.**

Useful pilot metrics vary by product and can include:
- handling time;
- completion rate;
- response quality;
- lead capture;
- operator hours saved;
- incident resolution time;
- workflow reliability;
- research time saved;
- approval latency;
- repeat usage or return rate.

## Pricing integrity

This public framework deliberately does not publish fictional list prices, discounts, ARR assumptions or unit economics. Exact prices should be introduced only when the package, verified capability, support scope and cost model are defined.
