# KING AI — Public Roadmap

**Platform:** https://www.kingai.work/

This roadmap communicates direction without exposing proprietary implementation or presenting future work as already released.

## Roadmap rule

A roadmap item is a direction, not a shipping claim. Current capability must be verified through the relevant official product surface, public release notes or an approved product conversation.

## Horizon 1 — Make every product independently useful

Primary goal: each active product should solve a real problem before ecosystem breadth is treated as an advantage.

Public priorities:

- make product roles easier to understand;
- improve onboarding and first-use value;
- separate current capability from future direction;
- improve mobile and desktop usability where applicable;
- strengthen reliability and recovery;
- maintain bilingual/public knowledge quality;
- build clearer product-specific conversion paths;
- keep legacy repositories from confusing customers.

### SEA / KINGAIBOT
Focus on clearer personal and business workflows, specialized AI roles, governed action and human control.

### KINGAI Office
Focus on everyday document, sheet, slide and PDF usefulness, compatibility, recovery and a coherent intelligent productivity experience.

### KINGAI Security
Focus on trustworthy endpoint protection, understandable findings, safe recovery and release validation.

### KINGAI OPS
Focus on useful infrastructure visibility, node/fleet operations, bounded automation and operational trust.

### Customer OS
Focus on approved knowledge, customer answers, lead capture, CRM follow-up, escalation and measurable business value.

### Online Tools
Focus on fast, search-friendly utilities that work with minimal setup and create natural product discovery.

### AI Life / Pet / Digital Life
Focus on persistence, return value, relationships, progression, safety and reasons to care about long-lived worlds and identities.

### Global Intelligence
Focus on freshness, methodology clarity, evidence provenance, useful country comparison and professional monitoring workflows.

## Horizon 2 — Build repeat-use and commercial depth

Primary goal: turn useful products into recurring relationships.

Directions may include:

- premium individual experiences;
- professional plans;
- team/business administration;
- organization workspaces;
- recurring monitoring and briefings;
- managed service and support;
- approved integrations;
- stronger cross-device continuity;
- clearer analytics around user outcomes;
- partner-led distribution;
- creator/content expansion for persistent consumer products.

No item in this section should be treated as universally available until released.

## Horizon 3 — Connect the strongest products

Primary goal: create ecosystem value only where users benefit from the connection.

Potential paths:

- Office + Security for protected productivity;
- Office + SEA/BOT for controlled workflow assistance;
- Customer OS + Office for customer-to-document workflows;
- Customer OS + SEA/BOT for governed customer operations;
- OPS + Security for operational protection;
- OPS + SEA/BOT for controlled infrastructure workflows;
- OS + Office + Security + BOT for AI-native computing;
- Pet + AI Life + Digital Life for persistent digital relationships;
- Global Intelligence + business workflows for international monitoring and research.

## Horizon 4 — Distribution and platform expansion

Longer-term opportunities may include:

- enterprise and institutional deployment;
- MSP and channel programs;
- OEM/device relationships;
- developer integrations;
- approved data/API products;
- international distribution;
- creator and entertainment partnerships;
- managed intelligence/research services;
- ecosystem account, entitlement and continuity improvements.

## Product discipline

Breadth is a risk as well as an opportunity. Public roadmap discipline therefore requires:

1. A product must have a clear customer.
2. A product must solve a recognizable problem.
3. Current capability must be separated from future direction.
4. A cross-product integration must produce user value, not just architectural elegance.
5. Commercial expansion should follow retention and usefulness.
6. Security and governance should mature with automation.
7. Products may be consolidated, renamed, paused or retired when that improves clarity.

## Metrics that matter

When verified and approved for disclosure, the most useful metrics include:

- activation;
- repeat use;
- retention;
- task/workflow completion;
- response and resolution quality;
- free-to-paid conversion;
- business opportunity conversion;
- recurring revenue;
- cross-product adoption driven by real need;
- reliability and recovery;
- support burden;
- partner-sourced adoption;
- persistent-world return behavior.

Public materials do not publish invented metrics to make the roadmap look more mature than it is.

## Core technology remains private

The roadmap intentionally omits proprietary algorithms, internal prompts, model routing, memory structures, private APIs, database design, production topology, credentials, security implementation and unreleased internal systems.
