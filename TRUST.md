# KING AI — Public Trust, Safety & Disclosure Standard

Trust is part of the product. This document explains what KING AI is willing to say publicly, what it intentionally keeps private and how users, businesses, partners and investors should interpret product claims.

**Official platform:** https://www.kingai.work/  
**Business & strategic contact:** vip@kingai.work

## 1. Public claims must be verifiable

KING AI public materials should distinguish clearly between:

- **Available now** — released and publicly verifiable;
- **Pilot / limited availability** — usable in a constrained or approved context;
- **Preview / pre-alpha / experimental** — not suitable for broad production assumptions;
- **Roadmap / direction** — an intended future path, not a current capability.

Marketing language must not silently turn a roadmap into a released feature.

## 2. We do not manufacture traction

Public materials should not invent or imply unverified:

- user counts or installations;
- revenue, ARR, MRR or growth rates;
- customer logos or enterprise deployments;
- signed partnerships;
- financing, valuation or investor participation;
- certifications or compliance status;
- market leadership;
- benchmark results that were not actually measured;
- reliability or availability commitments that were not formally offered.

When metrics become public, they should be traceable to a defined measurement and date.

## 3. Human authority remains explicit

KING AI's public product direction favors understandable authority rather than unrestricted automation.

Depending on the workflow, actions may be designed as:

- read;
- recommend;
- draft;
- execute within a defined scope;
- require approval;
- escalate;
- deny.

High-impact actions such as payments, publishing, deletion, access changes or irreversible operations should have clear ownership and, where appropriate, explicit approval paths.

## 4. Security is not a marketing adjective

Public security language should describe controls and boundaries, not claim perfect protection.

A credible evaluation should ask:

- what data enters the system;
- what is retained;
- which identities and permissions are used;
- what actions are allowed;
- what requires approval;
- what is logged;
- how errors are detected;
- how recovery works;
- who can override or stop automation.

No system should be presented as immune to failure, abuse or attack.

## 5. What public repositories may contain

Public repositories may contain approved material such as:

- product positioning;
- public documentation;
- public UI;
- public examples;
- sanitized datasets;
- release notes;
- public methodology;
- safe integration guidance;
- customer-safe knowledge;
- roadmap content clearly labeled as roadmap.

## 6. What public repositories must not contain

To protect users, systems and intellectual property, public repositories should not disclose:

- passwords, tokens, API keys or credentials;
- private keys or certificates;
- production network topology;
- internal infrastructure control paths;
- unpublished private endpoints;
- confidential customer information;
- private operational logs;
- proprietary algorithms;
- internal prompts or hidden policy instructions;
- model routing logic;
- private memory data structures;
- security detection or containment logic whose disclosure would increase risk;
- unreleased production data;
- private repository locations when disclosure creates unnecessary attack surface.

## 7. Public architecture should explain value, not expose the crown jewels

Users and investors should be able to understand:

- what problem a product solves;
- who it is for;
- what it can do at a product level;
- how authority is governed conceptually;
- how products relate to one another;
- what deployment choices are officially supported;
- what status is current;
- what evidence supports a claim.

They do not need internal implementation details to evaluate commercial value.

## 8. Data and intelligence products require provenance

Where KING AI publishes rankings, research, intelligence, safety findings or other derived information, public materials should make uncertainty visible.

Good practice includes:

- source attribution;
- date/freshness metadata;
- methodology versioning;
- missing data shown as missing rather than guessed;
- distinction between evidence volume and truth;
- separation of structural indicators from fast-moving signals;
- clear disclaimers for high-stakes decisions.

## 9. AI output is not automatically authoritative

AI can summarize, classify, compare, draft and assist. It can also be wrong.

Users should verify critical decisions against appropriate primary sources, experts, policies or operational evidence. Products should not encourage blind reliance merely because an answer sounds confident.

## 10. Commercial trust

Business buyers should be able to understand what they are paying for.

Potential commercial value may include:

- time savings;
- workflow continuity;
- reduced operational burden;
- managed support;
- better customer follow-up;
- clearer governance;
- security and recovery support;
- premium content or personalization;
- integration and implementation.

Pricing, service levels and deployment commitments should be tied to the actual offering, not broad AI claims.

## 11. Investor trust

Investors should evaluate KING AI on execution evidence, not only narrative.

Relevant diligence areas include:

- product usefulness;
- retention and repeat use;
- conversion and willingness to pay;
- reliability;
- customer acquisition efficiency;
- business pipeline quality;
- cross-product adoption;
- partner distribution;
- security and governance maturity;
- roadmap discipline;
- team execution and capital efficiency.

Private diligence may go deeper under appropriate confidentiality, but public repositories remain intentionally safe.

## 12. Responsible disclosure boundary

If a public-facing issue exposes a security weakness, credential, confidential data or implementation detail that could increase risk, the correct response is to reduce exposure first and publish only what is safe and necessary.

Transparency does not require publishing exploitable detail.

## 13. Product status over promotional wording

When product website language and verified release evidence conflict, the safer public interpretation should win until the discrepancy is corrected.

The objective is simple:

**Be ambitious about the future, precise about the present and protective of what should remain private.**
