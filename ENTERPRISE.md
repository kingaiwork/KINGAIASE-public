## Adoption pattern

A lower-risk enterprise adoption path is:

**1. Select one measurable workflow**  
Choose a problem with visible time, quality, response or operational cost.

**2. Define information boundaries**  
Specify which knowledge, systems and data are approved and which are not.

**3. Define authority**  
Separate read, recommend, prepare, approve and execute permissions.

**4. Pilot**  
Validate usefulness, failure modes, escalation and human experience.

**5. Measure**  
Track time saved, completion quality, conversion, response speed, issue rate or other relevant business outcomes.

**6. Expand only when justified**  
Add adjacent workflows or products when the first use case proves value.

## Governance principles

Enterprise AI should remain understandable even when the underlying intelligence improves.

KING AI public governance principles include:

- identity before authority;
- least privilege;
- explicit approval for higher-risk actions;
- auditable workflow state;
- human escalation;
- clear recovery or rollback paths where applicable;
- separation of public customer knowledge from private production information;
- roadmap claims separated from released capability.

## Buying criteria

A business evaluating KING AI should ask:

- What problem will this solve now?
- What is released versus roadmap?
- What information can the product access?
- What can it recommend versus execute?
- Where does human approval remain required?
- What is the recovery path?
- What evidence will show the pilot is working?
- What happens if an external AI provider changes?
- How will the product integrate with current work rather than create another silo?

These are healthy diligence questions and should be answered clearly.
